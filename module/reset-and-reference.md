# Cold boot and receiver references

The module exposes LCD_DATA0–15 on contacts 30–45. Each line shares a 10 kΩ, 1% on-module strap. The fitted word is 0x4017, selecting a 24 MHz oscillator and MMC0 first. The AM3354 samples these inputs at the rising edge of PWRONRSTn. The Z reset state on these pins leaves their level dependent on the actual pull circuit, leakage and carrier load. The same-export report lists each CPU ball, pull, connector contact and physical bias path. It also checks the two VDDSHV6 supply lands and the cold-reset source. [Physical reset and VREF paths](reset-vref.txt) · [TI AM335x datasheet, terminal functions and §5.7](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/am3354.pdf)

## Carrier load at the sampling edge

For VDDSHV6 in 3.3 V mode, TI specifies VIH ≥ 2.0 V and VIL ≤ 0.8 V. With the bank and high-side pull both settled at the minimum 3.135 V, and the resistor at 10.1 kΩ, the maximum total adverse DC load is 112.38 µA on a high strap and 79.21 µA on a low strap. Reserving 18 µA for AM3354 leakage with pulls inhibited leaves **94.38 µA sinking on a high strap** or **61.21 µA sourcing on a low strap** for the complete carrier, with zero additional noise reserve at those ceilings. This DC screen assumes valid local supply and ground and no active output contention. For each populated carrier input, include bias, connector protection, FPC and panel leakage under powered, ramping and unpowered conditions. An unpowered input clamp can invalidate a powered leakage specification. Check voltage at the CPU ball including rail and ground drop. Carrier leakage bounds, input capacitance, rail sequence and the physical CPU taps remain open.

Use the actual carrier capacitance and adverse load to check settling. For a constant rail and load, use V(t) = V∞ + (Vinitial − V∞) exp(−t/(Rth Ctotal)). Evaluate high and low straps from their worst initial states. For ramps or nonlinear clamps use the actual rail waveform and input model. Keep carrier-side drivers high impedance through cold reset and enable raster pinmux after sampling. Pulling panel reset low alone does not characterize its input clamps.

## First article

1. With known-good MMC0 media, fully cycle power with the panel connected. Capture VDDSHV6, PWRONRSTn, panel I/O supply and the strap voltages through the sampling edge. Check both reset assertion and release and verify MMC0 actually booted.
2. Read the **32-bit CONTROL_STATUS at 0x44E10040** through the debug path before changing pad configuration. Save the raw value, silicon revision, panel and supply conditions. Repeat after a full power removal with the panel disconnected. A warm reset retains the old sample.
3. Run `python3 check-reset-vref.py <pad-islands.json> --control-status <raw-word>` for each capture. Register bits 23:16 and 7:0 must decode to 0x4017. Other register fields may vary. Record both captures and boot results on the tested board revision. [TI TRM, Table 9-14 and boot configuration](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/spruh73q/spruh73q.pdf)

## Separate DDR references

On reads the AM3354 receiver uses J4 and the divider from the **switched CPU DDR rail**. On writes Micron H1 uses VREFDQ. Micron M8 uses VREFCA for command/address capture. Those two DRAM references are supplied by a second divider from the **direct DCDC3 DRAM rail**, with a 0 Ω branch to VREFCA. The copper report checks both divider supplies and returns, each bypass return and each receiver path. The 0 Ω link is a component between two distinct copper nets.

With two 1 kΩ, 1% resistors per divider, the unloaded DC ratio spans 0.495–0.505 of its local supply, versus the 0.49–0.51 receiver DC window. At exactly 1.35 V this leaves 6.75 mV per side before leakage, local rail/return drop and tracking error. For each receiver corner use its own supply and ground at the pins, its local effective bypass C, divider tolerance, input current and noise. Compare DQ and CA waveforms against the appropriate moving VREF and Micron AC/DC thresholds for the selected speed bin. Record valid dwell and source launch, setup and hold at each corner. Neither this divider calculation nor nominal trace flight establishes receiver margin. ### Command receiver screen on the routed module

25 routed synchronous commands were screened against CK at DDR3L-800 using the module’s trace and barrel flight estimates. The model assumes a nominal 1.35 V TI ZCZ transmitter fixture, IOCTRL 0x318 and a uniform 50 Ω line. The nine DRAM reference cases combine 1.323, 1.350 or 1.377 V at the DRAM with VREFCA at 0.49, 0.50 or 0.51 of that rail. All 32 sampled load/polarity combinations per line and reference case met the model’s slew, dwell and VIX screens. The intersection of these conditional source-capture windows is 700.630–1975.707 ps. Actual source launch phase, boot register values, real six-layer impedance, PVT, via discontinuities and reference noise remain open. Read DQ at CPU J4 and write DQ at the DRAM use separate timing analyses. Reproduce with check-command-receiver.py, the saved timing.csv and the SPRM552C TI IBIS archive. [Per-net results](command-receiver-screen.csv) · [TI AM3354 design resources](https://www.ti.com/product/AM3354)

The H1 VREFDQ pin has a dedicated underside 100 nF capacitor, C_H1_VREF. The reference and ground vias are centered in its two solder lands and require resin fill and copper caps. With the existing 100 nF C_VREF_DQ and a 500 Ω nominal divider source, these two capacitors contribute 100 μs nominal RC before the CA bypass, input loads, capacitor tolerance and rail ramp are included. Validate VREF startup and discharge against local VDDQ at both DRAM pins.
